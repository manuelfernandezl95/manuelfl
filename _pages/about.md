I am Manuel Fernández López, a condensed matter theorist working on strongly correlated quantum matter, topological phases, and unconventional superconductivity. I completed my PhD in Physics at the Autonomous University of Madrid (UAM) in 2024, under the supervision of Prof. Jaime Merino, with a thesis on fractionalized topological phases in strongly correlated materials with spin-orbit coupling. In September 2026 I joined the Lattice Field Theory Group at the Institute of Physics, NYCU, as a postdoctoral researcher, where I work with Prof. C.-J. David Lin translating computational techniques developed for strongly correlated, topologically ordered systems, parton-based mean-field methods and exact diagonalization, to open questions in hadron structure, while building complementary expertise in tensor network and DMRG approaches to lattice gauge theories.

My research so far has spanned interacting topological phases and quantum magnetism in decorated honeycomb lattices; unconventional and topological superconductivity arising from doping quantum spin liquids near flat bands, including the identification of localized Majorana edge modes relevant to topological quantum computing; fractionalized topological semimetals and the Weyl-Mott metal-insulator transition, where non-local correlations give rise to spinon Fermi arcs; and, most recently, the use of Green's function zeroes as a diagnostic tool for topological strongly correlated phases, including an ongoing application to Kitaev materials such as α-RuCl₃.

Alongside my research, I co-supervise graduate students, act as corresponding author guiding manuscripts through the full peer-review process, and referee for Physical Review B and Physical Review Letters.
